**ISP** stands for **Internet Service Provider**. It is a company or organization that provides you with access to the internet. They act as the intermediary between your device (computer, phone, etc.) and the wider internet, allowing you to send and receive data online.

### Key Roles of an ISP:

1.  **Internet Access**: ISPs offer various methods of connecting you to the internet, such as:
    
    -   **Dial-up** (older technology, very slow)
        
    -   **Broadband** (e.g., DSL, cable, fiber optics)
        
    -   **Mobile Networks** (e.g., 4G, 5G)
        
    -   **Satellite** (for rural or remote areas)
        
2.  **Assigning IP Addresses**: They assign you an IP address (either dynamic or static) to identify your device on the network.
    
3.  **Routing Traffic**: ISPs route your internet traffic, meaning they manage the flow of data between your device and the websites or services you access.
    
4.  **Additional Services**: Many ISPs also provide additional services, such as:
    
    -   **Web hosting**
        
    -   **Email services**
        
    -   **Cloud storage**
        
    -   **Streaming services**
        
    -   **VPNs** (for private browsing)
        

### Examples of ISPs:

-   **Comcast** (Xfinity)
    
-   **AT&T**
    
-   **Verizon**
    
-   **BT (British Telecom)**
    
-   **Sky**
    
-   **Vodafone**
    

In essence, your ISP is the company that gives you the "pipe" or connection to the internet and manages your access to online resources.
