### 1\. How Internet Packets are Transmitted

The internet is based on **packet-switching** technology. Here’s how a packet travels:

1.  **Breaking Data into Packets**  
    When you request a website, your computer breaks the data into smaller **packets** (small chunks of data with headers that define where they’re from, where they’re going, and the order to reassemble them).
    
2.  **IP Routing**  
    Each packet is tagged with an **IP address** (destination address) and sent via a **router** to its next destination. Routers are devices that look at the destination IP and forward the packet along the correct path while doing [[De-encapsulation & Re-encapsulation]].  
    The packet might pass through many routers before it reaches its destination.
    
3.  **Transmission Medium**  
    The packets travel through a **combination of physical cables** (fiber-optic, copper, or even wireless links like 5G) or **satellite links**.

### 2\. The Backbone of the Internet: Fiber-Optic Cables

The **majority of internet traffic** today is transmitted through **fiber-optic cables**, which form the backbone of the internet. Here's how they work:

1.  **Fiber Optics**  
    Fiber-optic cables use **light** to transmit data. They contain strands of glass or plastic that carry light signals over long distances. The data is encoded in the **light** by changing the frequency (modulating the light).
    
2.  **Advantages of Fiber-Optics**
    
    -   **Speed:** Fiber-optic cables can transmit data at speeds close to the speed of light, which is about **300,000 kilometers per second** in the fiber. This is **extremely fast** compared to older technologies.
        
    -   **Capacity:** Fiber cables can carry **massive amounts of data** simultaneously, using multiple wavelengths of light (dense wavelength division multiplexing, or DWDM). This means you can send many **channels of data** at once over the same cable.
        
    -   **Low Latency:** Because of light's high speed, fiber offers **minimal delay**. This is why it's used in everything from **data centers** to **international backbone connections**.
        
3.  **Submarine Fiber-Optic Cables**  
    The global internet infrastructure is **interconnected by submarine fiber-optic cables**. These cables stretch across **oceans** and connect **continents**. They are laid on the seabed or sometimes buried underground on land.
    
    -   For example, major cables like the **Transatlantic cable** link **North America** and **Europe**, while the **Transpacific cables** link **Asia** and **North America**.
        
    -   These cables are critical for the **global internet** and are responsible for carrying **over 99%** of international data.
        
    
    **Example of submarine cables:**
    
    -   **MAREA**: A transatlantic cable connecting **Virginia, USA** to **Bilbao, Spain**.
        
    -   **SEA-ME-WE**: A series of cables connecting **South East Asia** to **Europe**.

### 3\. Why ADSL is Slower than Fiber

**ADSL (Asymmetric Digital Subscriber Line)** is much slower than **fiber-optic** for several reasons:

1.  **Transmission Medium**
    
    -   **ADSL** uses **copper telephone lines** (twisted pair cables) to transmit data. Copper is much less efficient for transmitting high-speed data over long distances compared to **fiber-optic cables**.
        
    -   **Fiber-optic** cables transmit data via light, which moves much faster and can carry far more data simultaneously compared to copper's electrical signals.
        
2.  **Distance Limitation**
    
    -   **ADSL** performance degrades with distance from the local exchange (the central hub where your ISP connects to the broader network).
        
    -   For ADSL, **distance matters** — the further you are from the exchange, the slower the speeds (due to signal attenuation and noise).
        
    -   **Fiber** doesn't degrade in performance over distance the same way; the signal doesn’t weaken like electrical signals do in copper.
        
3.  **Speed Limitations**
    
    -   **ADSL** can provide speeds ranging from **1 Mbps to 24 Mbps** depending on how close you are to the exchange.
        
    -   **Fiber-optic connections** (like **FTTH — Fiber to the Home**) can provide **gigabit speeds** (1000 Mbps) or more, with some advanced fiber connections even supporting **10 Gbps**.
        
4.  **Interference**
    
    -   **Copper cables (ADSL)** are more prone to **electromagnetic interference** (EMI), which affects data transmission speeds and reliability.
        
    -   **Fiber-optic** cables are immune to electromagnetic interference because they use light instead of electrical signals.

### 4\. Why This is All Possible: Speed of Light

The **speed of light** is the key reason **fiber-optic cables** are so fast. Here's why it matters:

1.  **Speed of Light in Fiber**
    
    -   The speed of light in a vacuum is about **300,000 km/s**.
        
    -   When light travels through a fiber-optic cable, it slows down slightly due to the medium (glass), but it still travels incredibly fast compared to the speed of electrical signals in copper cables.
        
    -   This high-speed transfer allows for extremely **low latency**, meaning **minimal delay** between sending and receiving data.
        
2.  **Long-Distance Transmission**
    
    -   In fiber-optic cables, **light** can travel over thousands of kilometers without significant loss of data quality.
        
    -   Copper cables used in **ADSL** suffer from significant **signal degradation** over distance, making them slower and limiting how far they can reach efficiently.

### 5\. Biggest ISPs in Terms of Infrastructure

The biggest ISPs in terms of **global infrastructure** usually own vast amounts of **fiber-optic cables**, data centers, and interconnection points. Here are the top players:

1.  **AT&T** (USA)  
    AT&T is one of the largest ISPs globally, with extensive **fiber-optic networks** and large data centers. They operate a huge portion of the **land-based backbone** in the USA.
    
2.  **Verizon** (USA)  
    Verizon’s **Fios** fiber-optic network is one of the largest in the US, offering **high-speed internet** and **fiber-optic backhaul** services across the country.
    
3.  **Comcast** (USA)  
    Comcast is a major player in North America, with a vast network infrastructure. They are involved in both **last-mile broadband** and **large-scale data transport**.
    
4.  **China Telecom** (China)  
    As the **largest ISP in China**, China Telecom has one of the largest **fiber-optic networks** in the world. They also manage significant **international submarine cables**.
    
5.  **NTT Communications** (Japan)  
    NTT operates Japan’s largest network and provides **fiber-to-the-home** (FTTH) services as well as **global data center services**.
    
6.  **Vodafone** (UK)  
    Vodafone operates a large fiber-optic network, particularly in **Europe**, and provides both **consumer broadband** and **business networking solutions**.
    
7.  **Orange** (France)  
    Orange has a large **fiber-optic backbone** in Europe and significant **international infrastructure**, especially across Africa.
    
8.  **Telefonica** (Spain)  
    Telefonica operates an extensive **fiber-optic network** in Europe and Latin America, with large backbone and last-mile connectivity.
    
9.  **Level 3 Communications** (now part of **CenturyLink**)  
    Known for its extensive **global fiber network** that connects continents and provides backbone services to other ISPs and large businesses.
    
10.  **Telecom Italia (TIM)** (Italy)  
    Telecom Italia has one of the largest **fiber-optic infrastructures** in Europe, providing both **consumer broadband** and **global backbone** services.
